# Observational causal inference (Milestone 5)

Status: `IMPLEMENTED`, `TESTED`, `SIMULATED` as of Milestone 5 — see
[../evidence/capability-status.md](../evidence/capability-status.md). Numerical evidence:
[../evidence/m5-observational-causal-inference-results.md](../evidence/m5-observational-causal-inference-results.md)
(generated by `python scripts/generate_m5_evidence.py`, about six minutes). Section references
("results §n") point to that file.

M4 identified effects by *measuring* the confounders (backdoor adjustment). M5 covers designs that
identify effects without measuring them, by exploiting **time** (difference-in-differences, event
studies, synthetic control) or a **threshold** (regression discontinuity). Each trades the
unconfoundedness assumption for a different, equally untestable one — parallel trends, a faithful
donor combination, continuity at the cutoff — and the methods are not interchangeable.

## Scope and boundaries

In scope: two-period and panel DiD; staggered adoption (Callaway & Sant'Anna 2021); static and
dynamic TWFE with an exact negative-weight diagnostic; event studies with pointwise intervals and
simultaneous bands; pre-trend tests; wild cluster bootstrap for few clusters; synthetic control with
placebo and sensitivity analyses; sharp RDD with MSE-optimal bandwidth, robust bias-corrected
inference, density and covariate tests, placebo cutoffs and donut checks.

Out of scope (recorded in the roadmap): interrupted time series, fuzzy RDD, regression kink designs,
synthetic DiD and augmented/penalised synthetic control, conformal inference for synthetic control,
honest-DiD sensitivity bounds (Rambachan & Roth), propensity-score matching (still deferred from M4),
and anything belonging to experimentation infrastructure (M6+).

## Synthetic data (additive extension of the M2 foundation)

Three new, self-contained generators in `src/decision_intelligence/data/`. None reads or modifies an
M2 table, generator, schema or evidence file; an integration test confirms the M2 build is
byte-identical before and after they run.

| Module | NexaMarket question | Ground truth | Deliberate failure switches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rollout_panel.py` | Effect of a delivery-service policy rolled out to regional markets in waves | Per market-period effect; ATT(g, t); overall and event-time ATT | anticipation, differential trends (parallel trends fails), spillovers onto never-treated markets |
| `synthetic_control_panel.py` | Effect of a flagship pricing policy launched in one region | Effect path; generating donor weights | treated region outside the donors' convex hull; contaminated (spilled-over) donors |
| `threshold_assignment.py` | Effect of the loyalty tier granted at engagement score ≥ 60 | τ(x) for every customer; τ(c); population ATE; ATT | manipulation of the reported score; a compound treatment at the same cutoff |

**Timing convention** (all DiD/event-study code): periods are integers; cohort `g` is the first
treated period (0 = never); treatment is absorbing; event time `e = t − g`, so `e = 0` is the first
treated period and `e = −1` the reference. With `k` periods of anticipation the base period moves to
`g − 1 − k`.

## 1. Difference-in-differences and event studies — `causal_inference/did.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | How much did the delivery policy change weekly revenue per customer in markets that adopted it? |
| Estimand | ATT(g, t) for each cohort and period; overall ATT (average over treated market-periods, i.e. cohort-size-weighted); event-time ATT(e) (cohort-size-weighted over cohorts observed at e). |
| Identification | (1) parallel trends between each cohort and its comparison group; (2) no anticipation before the base period (or a stated window); (3) no spillovers onto comparison units; (4) absorbing treatment, balanced panel. None of (1)–(3) is testable for post-treatment periods. |
| Formulation | 2×2: difference in mean changes. Callaway & Sant'Anna: `ATT(g,t) = E[Y_t − Y_{g−1−k} | G = g] − E[Y_t − Y_{g−1−k} | C]`, `C` = never-treated or not-yet-treated (cohort > max(t, base) + k, excluding g); universal base period. Static TWFE `Y = α_i + λ_t + βD + u`; dynamic TWFE with relative-time indicators (reference −1, endpoints binned). |
| Implementation | TWFE via M3 `fit_ols` with unit/period dummies, unit-clustered CR1 SEs, t(G − 1). CS inference from exact unit-level influence functions (each cell is a difference of two independent means; per-group `n/(n−1)` factors reproduce the Welch variance), **conditional on cohort membership**; a unit bootstrap through the M3 primitive is available. |
| TWFE decomposition | If `Y_it = α_i + λ_t + τ_it D_it + ε_it` in a balanced panel, by Frisch–Waugh–Lovell `β̂ = Σ_{D=1} w_it τ_it + Σ D̃ ε / Σ D̃²` with `w_it = D̃_it / Σ D̃²` (two-way demeaned treatment). The weights depend only on adoption dates and panel length; they sum to 1; under common timing they are all equal (TWFE = overall ATT). Under staggered adoption some are negative: in the canonical panel **22% of treated market-periods (a count share)** carry negative weight, totalling −0.047 of weight mass, and with the true effects the weights imply 3.157 — a *different estimand* from the overall ATT 4.028. Non-parallel trends or spillovers add further terms not covered by the decomposition. Monte Carlo rows for TWFE are therefore labelled "deviation from the overall ATT", not bias for TWFE's own estimand. |
| Event studies | Pointwise 95% intervals per event time and a **sup-t simultaneous band** (Montiel Olea & Plagborg-Møller 2019) over the reported window (6 leads, all lags), from the influence-function covariance. The band is a **nominal 95%** simultaneous band. Over 400 panels, pointwise intervals cover the whole path jointly in only 0.492 (Monte Carlo 95% interval [0.444, 0.541]); the band's observed joint coverage is **0.920 (Monte Carlo 95% interval [0.889, 0.943])** — i.e. this design exhibits **undercoverage**, and nominal 95% joint coverage is **not guaranteed**. The shortfall is plausibly concentrated at the long horizons, which rest on one cohort of 15 markets (not separately verified; results §2b). |
| Pre-trend test | Joint Wald test on the window's leads, referred to the finite-cluster F distribution `(G − q)/(q(G − 1))·W ~ F(q, G − q)`. Size over 1,000 panels per row (results §2b): 0.054 [0.042, 0.070] with 60 units, 0.049 with 40, 0.045 with 120 — calibrated — but **0.102 [0.085, 0.122] with 20 units (5 per group)**, where a warning is now issued. Testing *all* leads with a χ² reference is badly over-sized (0.339 with 60 units, 0.986 with 20). |
| Reference / analytic checks | 2×2 = TWFE in a two-period panel = Welch on unit changes; TWFE estimate and clustered SE = statsmodels dummy OLS; noiseless panels: CS recovers every ATT(g, t) exactly and TWFE = Σ w τ exactly; under common timing CS event study = saturated dynamic TWFE exactly; each CS cell's influence-function SE = its Welch SE; an **independent pandas re-implementation** of ATT(g, t) (never- and not-yet-treated) and of the overall aggregation agrees to 1e-12 on noisy data (tests). |
| Baselines | Static TWFE; dynamic TWFE; naive comparisons. |
| Evaluation (150 panels per design, results §2) | Valid staggered design: CS bias −0.019 ± 0.021, coverage 0.967 [0.924, 0.986]; static TWFE deviates by −0.878 ± 0.013 from the overall ATT (coverage 0.013). Common timing: TWFE and CS unbiased, coverage 0.960 and 0.947. Influence-function intervals vs unit bootstrap (100 panels): coverage 0.990 vs 1.000, reported SE 0.260 vs 0.343 against an empirical SD of 0.247 — the bootstrap is conservative because it randomises cohort sizes fixed by design. |
| Failure modes | Ignored 2-period anticipation: −1.130 (modelled: −0.010); spillovers: −0.630 staggered, −1.785 common timing; violated parallel trends: +0.536. **Dynamic TWFE event studies under staggering** report spurious pre-trends (0.853 rejection although trends are parallel; Sun & Abraham 2021) — flagged. Under the parallel-trends violation static TWFE *looks* acceptable (coverage 0.89) only because its trend bias offsets its negative-weight deviation — a coincidence, not robustness. |
| Interpretation | Report the CS overall and event-time ATTs, read event paths with the simultaneous band while remembering it under-covers here (observed 0.920 against nominal 0.95), use TWFE only with a single adoption date. A non-rejected pre-trend test is consistent with parallel trends, never proof of it. |
| Decision relevance | Early adopters gain most and effects grow for a month; the pooled TWFE number would understate the policy's value by about a fifth. |

### Few-cluster inference — `causal_inference/cluster_inference.py`

Wild cluster restricted bootstrap (WCR; Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008): the null is imposed by
regressing `y − β₀x_j` on the other regressors; restricted residuals are flipped cluster by cluster
with Rademacher (±1) or Webb six-point weights; each draw is studentised with the same CR1 variance
and finite-sample factor `G/(G−1)·(n−1)/(n−k)` as the observed statistic; the p-value is the share
of `|t*| ≥ |t|` (symmetric). All `2^G` (Rademacher) or `6^G` (Webb) sign patterns are enumerated
when that does not exceed the requested draws. Confidence intervals invert the test with common
weight draws. Vectorised through Frisch–Waugh–Lovell.

Reference: identical p-values to a brute-force implementation that refits every enumerated sign
pattern with M3 `fit_ols` (tested), and to `wildboottest` 0.3.2 (`scripts/crosscheck_m5_external.py`,
12 clusters, full enumeration of 4,096 patterns, both p = 0.2051 — its default WCR with Rademacher
weights and the same CR1 correction, i.e. an equivalent procedure).

Evidence (null effect, AR(1) ρ = 0.7, 500 panels per row; results §3): classical OLS SEs reject
29–33% of the time (serial correlation ignored); with 5+ treated clusters CR1 and WCR are near
nominal. **Unreliable regimes:** with 2 treated clusters of 10, CR1 rejects 15.8% and WCR
0.2–1.2%; with 1 treated cluster, CR1 rejects **47.6%** and WCR 0.0–0.4% — no power. No
cluster-based method is trustworthy with one or two treated clusters (MacKinnon & Webb 2017).
`wild_cluster_bootstrap` now reports how many clusters carry the tested regressor and warns at three
or fewer (tested).

## 2. Synthetic control — `causal_inference/synthetic_control.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | What would the treated region's revenue have been without the pricing policy? |
| Estimand | The treated unit's effect path `τ_t = Y_1t(1) − Y_1t(0)` and its post-period average. A single-unit, not a population, effect. |
| Identification | A convex combination of donors reproduces the treated unit's untreated path (plausible under a factor model when its loadings lie in the donors' convex hull and the pre-period is long); donors unaffected by the treatment; no anticipation. |
| Formulation | `w = argmin ||y_1,pre − Y_0,pre w||²` s.t. `w ≥ 0, Σw = 1`; counterfactual `Y_0 w`; gap = estimated effect. |
| Implementation | SLSQP, then an exact **active-set refinement**, then **certification** by the Frank–Wolfe duality gap `g'w − min_j g_j` (an upper bound on the distance to the optimal objective); raises if the relative gap exceeds 1e-8. Weights are feasible by construction (non-negative, sum to 1). |
| Diagnostics | Pre-RMSPE relative to the pre-period SD (warning above 0.5); share of pre-periods in which the treated outcome lies outside that period's donor range — a **necessary** condition for convex-hull membership, not a sufficient one (the pre-period fit is the operative check); effective number of donors; leave-one-out over positively weighted donors; in-time placebo. |
| Reference checks | Exact recovery of known convex weights; brute-force simplex grid; the certificate rejects weights shifted by 1e-4 between donors (tests). |
| Baseline | Naive DiD against the donor average (ignores differing factor loadings): RMSE 2.67 vs 0.72 for synthetic control. |
| Inference | In-space placebos: each donor treated in turn (true treated unit excluded from their pools); rank of the post/pre RMSPE ratio; `rank/(J + 1)` with smallest attainable value `1/(J + 1)` (0.032 with 30 donors); optional exclusion of placebos whose pre-period **MSPE** exceeds a multiple of the treated unit's (Abadie et al. 2010). **An exact permutation p-value only if the treated unit had been chosen at random**; otherwise a descriptive rank. With no effect the treated region ranks first 1.7% of the time; with the effect 98.3%. |
| Evaluation (60 panels) | Valid: bias +0.149 ± 0.091, RMSE 0.72. Outside the convex hull: RMSE 16.9 — flagged in 100% of fits. Contaminated donors: bias −0.469 ± 0.122 — flagged in **0%** of fits. |
| Failure modes | Extrapolation outside the hull; donor contamination; short pre-periods (warned below 10); weights are *not identified* when donors are collinear in factor space — the counterfactual path, not the weights, is the result. |
| Interpretation | Report the gap path with its pre-period fit, the placebo distribution (with the exclusion rule used) and leave-one-out sensitivity; never a single "p < 0.05". |

## 3. Regression discontinuity — `causal_inference/rdd.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | What does the loyalty tier do to next-quarter spend? |
| Estimand | **τ(c)**, the effect for customers *at* the score cutoff. In the canonical data τ(c) = 3.0 while the population-average effect is 2.20 and the tier members' average 3.71: the RDD estimate must not be reported as either. |
| Identification | Continuity of both potential-outcome regressions at the cutoff — violated by sorting/manipulation of the running variable or by any other treatment switching on at the same cutoff; known, sharp cutoff. |
| Formulation | Local polynomial on each side, triangular kernel, bandwidth h: `y = a + τD + Σ_p [b_p (x − c)^p + d_p D (x − c)^p]`; effective sample = observations with `|x − c| < h`. |
| Implementation | Weighted least squares via M3 `fit_ols` on √w-transformed data (HC1). Bandwidth: Imbens & Kalyanaraman (2012) MSE-optimal for local linear. **Inference: robust bias-corrected** (Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik 2014) with pilot bandwidth = h (ρ = 1), which equals the local-quadratic fit at h; the conventional local-linear interval is also reported. |
| Validity checks | McCrary (2008) density test; approximate binomial window test; continuity of a pre-determined covariate; placebo cutoffs (data from one side only); donut estimates; bandwidth sensitivity; global polynomials as a cautionary comparison. |
| Reference checks | Local linear and quadratic estimates and SEs = statsmodels WLS (tests). Against `rdrobust` 2.1.1 with **equivalent settings** (same h, b = h, triangular kernel, p = 1, `vce="hc1"`): identical estimates to 10 decimals, identical effective sample sizes (1002, 703), SEs within 0.01% (HC1 degrees-of-freedom factor pooled across sides here vs per side in `rdrobust`); with `rdrobust`'s default nearest-neighbour variance the robust SE differs by 1.3%. IK bandwidth: scale-equivariant in x and invariant to affine y (tests); against a Monte Carlo oracle (results §7) IK averages 8.53 while the MSE keeps falling to the top of the grid (h = 14) — IK is smaller than the oracle (MSE 0.080 vs 0.061), trading efficiency for less smoothing bias. The PyPI `rdd` package's IK returns about half this bandwidth because it floors m₃² at 0.01 (scale-dependent; development check). |
| Evaluation (300 datasets) | Valid design: conventional coverage 0.933 [0.899, 0.956], robust 0.930 [0.895, 0.954]; at a placebo cutoff where the outcome curves sharply the conventional interval rejects 9.3% of the time vs 4.0% for robust. Global quartic: bias −0.318. Manipulation: robust bias +0.781, detected by McCrary and the covariate test 100% of the time. Compound treatment: bias +1.96, flagged only at chance rates (density 3.3%, covariate 4.7%). |
| Canonical draw | The canonical seed's robust estimate is 1.74 [0.93, 2.55], about 3 SE below the truth; an independent `rdrobust` fit agrees (robust CI [0.975, 2.599]). Reported as generated; repeated sampling shows the procedure is calibrated. |
| Failure modes | Sorting; compound treatments; global high-order polynomials (6.80 / 3.01 / 2.29 / 2.00 for orders 1 / 2 / 4 / 6 on the canonical data); donuts that extrapolate across a wide hole. |
| Interpretation | An effect for marginal customers near the threshold. Extending the tier to customers far below the cutoff is a different question RDD does not answer. |

## Detectable and undetectable violations

| Violation | Design | Detectable with the available observations? | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anticipation | DiD | Yes, if it begins before the reference period: leads move | CS pre-test rejects 0.973; bias −1.130 removed by modelling it |
| Non-parallel trends | DiD | Only partly: an implication is testable in the pre-period, with low power | Pre-test detects 0.207 of the time although CS is biased +0.536 |
| Spillovers onto controls | DiD | Not under common timing (pre-test at its null rate, 0.033, bias −1.785); only incidentally under staggering, where later cohorts' leads are contaminated (0.773) | results §2 |
| Donor contamination | Synthetic control | No: it occurs only after treatment; pre-period fit is unaffected | 0% of contaminated fits flagged; bias −0.469 |
| Treated unit outside the donors' hull | Synthetic control | Yes, through pre-period fit | 100% flagged |
| Threshold manipulation | RDD | Yes when it moves the density or covariates | McCrary and covariate tests 100% |
| Simultaneous policy at the cutoff | RDD | No: density and covariates stay continuous | flagged at chance rates (3.3%, 4.7%); bias +1.96 |

Undetectable violations can only be ruled out by knowledge of the design (who could be affected,
which other rules use the same threshold), not by the data.

## Decision relevance

Each design answers a narrower question than a naive comparison appears to: the CS ATT applies to
the markets that adopted, when they adopted; synthetic control speaks for one region; RDD speaks for
customers at the threshold. Their uncertainty statements are only as good as their identifying
assumptions, which the diagnostics can challenge but never confirm.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic ground truth validates code and logic, not real-world validity.
- CS inference is conditional on cohort composition (exact for the synthetic design, where cohort
  sizes are fixed); no covariates in the CS estimator (unconditional parallel trends only).
- Pre-trend testing is limited to a lead window, over-sized with ~5 units per group, and low-powered;
  no honest-DiD sensitivity bounds. The nominal 95% simultaneous bands under-cover (observed joint coverage 0.920, Monte Carlo
  95% interval [0.889, 0.943]), particularly at long horizons; nominal coverage is not guaranteed.
- Wild bootstrap: unreliable with one or two treated clusters (documented, demonstrated, warned).
- Synthetic control: outcome-lag predictors only (no covariate matching or predictor weighting V);
  no conformal or other model-based interval; the hull diagnostic is necessary, not sufficient.
- RDD: sharp designs only; HC1 rather than nearest-neighbour variance; robust bias correction with
  ρ = 1 only; IK rather than the CCT bandwidth selector; the McCrary test assumes a smooth density
  and cannot detect two-way sorting.
- External cross-checks against `rdrobust`, `wildboottest` and `rdd` are development checks
  (`scripts/crosscheck_m5_external.py`), not CI tests; those packages are not dependencies.
