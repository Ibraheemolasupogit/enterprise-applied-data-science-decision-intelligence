# Causal foundations, DAGs and backdoor adjustment (Milestone 4)

Status: `IMPLEMENTED`, `TESTED`, `SIMULATED` as of Milestone 4 — see
[../evidence/capability-status.md](../evidence/capability-status.md). Numerical evidence:
[../evidence/m4-causal-foundations-results.md](../evidence/m4-causal-foundations-results.md)
(generated by `python scripts/generate_m4_evidence.py`).

This is the methodology of record for `src/decision_intelligence/causal_inference/`. It answers one
question, *what would retention have been without the retention offer?*, on the M2 synthetic
campaign, and documents each method against Master Brief §17 (question, estimand, data-generating
assumptions, identification assumptions, formulation, implementation, baseline, evaluation,
uncertainty, failure modes, interpretation, decision relevance).

## Scope

In scope (Brief §6.3 and the backdoor-adjustment rows of §6.4): potential outcomes; ATE, ATT,
conditional effects; DAGs, d-separation, confounders, mediators, colliders, backdoor criterion;
identification assumptions (consistency, exchangeability, positivity, SUTVA); regression
adjustment, inverse probability weighting and doubly robust AIPW; overlap, balance and weight
diagnostics; negative controls and a DAG falsification test; sensitivity to unmeasured
confounding; deliberate failure cases.

Out of scope: difference-in-differences, event studies, interrupted time series, synthetic control
and regression discontinuity (M5); experimentation infrastructure (M6+); causal ML and
meta-learners. **Propensity-score matching** (Brief §6.4) is deliberately not implemented: valid
inference for matching estimators needs the Abadie–Imbens variance rather than the bootstrap, and is
recorded as an open item in the roadmap rather than shipped without honest uncertainty.

M3 primitives are reused, not reimplemented: `fit_logistic` (propensity and binary outcome models,
including its exact separation check), `fit_ols`, `two_sample_t_test`, `bootstrap_ci` (resampling
customers) and the shared validation helpers.

## The case: the M2 retention offer

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | What is the effect of the retention offer on 90-day retention? |
| Estimand | ATE `E[Y(1) − Y(0)]` and ATT `E[Y(1) − Y(0) | T=1]`, Y = `retained_90d`, on the probability (risk-difference) scale. |
| Data-generating process (known) | `T ~ Bernoulli(sigmoid(−2 + 4·latent_value))`; retention probability `= sigmoid(−0.3 + segment offset + 1.6·latent_value) + 0.08·T`, no clipping binding. Hence ATE = ATT = every subgroup CATE = **0.08**. |
| Causal structure | DAG read off the generator (`campaign.campaign_dag()`): `latent_value` causes treatment and churn; segment causes `latent_value` and churn; treatment affects retention only through churn time; billing activity is a descendant of churn. |
| Ground truth use | `dataset.ground_truth` is attached *after* all estimation, solely to evaluate recovery. The logged `assignment_propensity` is used only in regime C, where it is the stated design information. |

Three information regimes are analysed because identification depends on what is measured:

| Regime | Adjustment information | DAG verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | `latent_value` (+ segment) | backdoor criterion satisfied → identified |
| B | observed proxies only (segment, price sensitivity, channel, region) — the M2 docs call `latent_value` hidden | backdoor path `treated ← latent_value → churn_time → retained` open → **not identified** |
| C | logged true assignment probability | identified by weighting (a known propensity is a balancing score) without measuring the confounder |

M2 records `latent_value` in the `customers` table although its documentation calls it hidden; M4
makes the distinction explicit through regimes A and B rather than silently choosing one.

**Regime C is a design regime, not an observational one.** It is valid only because the M2 targeting
*randomised* each customer with a probability it recorded. Ordinary observational data contain no
such probabilities; a logged model score used by a deterministic rule is not a propensity (and would
imply a positivity violation). Regime C therefore illustrates what a stochastic, logged targeting
policy buys — it must not be read as a way to analyse ordinary observational data.

## Identification assumptions

| Assumption | Meaning | Status in the M2 campaign | Demonstrated failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | Observed Y equals the potential outcome under the received treatment; one version of treatment | Holds by construction (one offer) | Not simulated; documented |
| No interference (SUTVA) | A unit's outcome does not depend on others' treatments | Holds by construction | `scenarios.interference`: difference in means recovers the direct effect (1.0), not the treat-everyone effect (3.0) |
| Conditional exchangeability | `Y(t) ⊥ T | X` for the adjustment set; on the DAG, the backdoor criterion | Holds for regime A; fails for regime B | Regime B (+0.028 bias); `scenarios.m_bias`; post-treatment adjustment |
| Positivity | `0 < e(X) < 1` wherever we target | Holds: e ∈ [0.12, 0.90] | `scenarios.positivity` (ESS collapse, AIPW RMSE 9.7); perfect prediction raises `PositivityError` |

## 1. DAGs and identification — `causal_inference/dag.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | Which variables must, and must not, be adjusted for? |
| Formulation | d-separation by the moralised-ancestral-graph criterion (Lauritzen et al. 1990), cross-checked by explicit path enumeration with the collider/non-collider blocking rule; backdoor criterion = no descendant of T in Z and `T ⊥ Y | Z` in the graph with T's out-edges removed; exhaustive search for minimal valid sets among *measured* variables; role classification (confounder, mediator, post-treatment, collider on a T–Y path). |
| Reference | networkx `is_d_separator` (dev-only oracle): agreement on >1,000 random queries; the two internal implementations agree on every random graph; classic textbook graphs (Pearl's z1–z3 example, M-bias, mediation). |
| Evaluation on M2 | Only minimal valid set among measured variables: `{latent_value}`. Implied independencies tested on data: LR test of `treated ⊥ segment, price_sensitivity, channel, region | latent_value` — χ² = 5.80 on 9 df, p = 0.76 (not rejected). |
| Failure modes | A DAG is an assumption; data can refute some implications but never confirm the graph. Cycles, self-loops and unknown nodes raise. |
| Interpretation | "Adjust for everything pre-treatment" is wrong: colliders (M-bias) and descendants of treatment must be excluded, and a set of plausible proxies can still leave a path open. |

## 2. Estimands — `causal_inference/estimands.py`

`PotentialOutcomes` holds both potential outcomes (simulation only) and computes ATE, ATT, ATC and
CATE, and the exact identity *naive difference = ATT + selection bias*,
`selection bias = E[Y(0) | T=1] − E[Y(0) | T=0]`. Estimators never read it.

## 3. Estimators — `causal_inference/estimators.py`

Arm-specific outcome models (logistic for binary Y, OLS otherwise) avoid imposing a constant effect;
propensity by main-effects logistic regression.

| Estimator | Formula (ATE) | ATT | Consistent if | Uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Naive | `ȳ₁ − ȳ₀` | — | T independent of potential outcomes | Welch (M3) |
| Regression adjustment (g-computation) | `mean(μ̂₁(X) − μ̂₀(X))` | `mean_{T=1}(Y − μ̂₀(X))` | outcome models correct | nonparametric bootstrap of customers (M3 `bootstrap_ci`), percentile |
| IPW, Horvitz–Thompson | `mean(TY/ê − (1−T)Y/(1−ê))` | — | propensity correct | influence function or bootstrap |
| IPW, Hájek | normalised-weight means | treated mean − control mean weighted by `ê/(1−ê)` | propensity correct | influence function or bootstrap |
| AIPW | `mean(μ̂₁ − μ̂₀ + T(Y−μ̂₁)/ê − (1−T)(Y−μ̂₀)/(1−ê))` | `Σ[T(Y−μ̂₀) − (1−T)ê/(1−ê)(Y−μ̂₀)]/n₁` | either model correct | influence function or bootstrap |

**Influence-function standard errors.**

| Estimator | Influence function used | Validity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPW, estimated propensity (ATE and ATT) | includes the M-estimation correction for the logistic propensity fit, `φ* = φ + H I⁻¹ sᵢ` (Lunceford & Davidian 2004) | asymptotically exact if the propensity model is correct; matches statsmodels' joint-GMM SEs for the ATE (within 3%, tested) and is calibrated against Monte Carlo for the ATT |
| IPW, known propensity (regime C) | uncorrected | exact: nothing is estimated |
| AIPW | nuisance models treated as fixed | asymptotically exact when both models are correct; under single misspecification use `se_method="bootstrap"` (in the M2 replicates the fixed-nuisance SE still tracks the empirical SD) |
| Regression adjustment | — | nonparametric bootstrap |

An earlier version omitted the propensity correction for IPW: its ATE intervals were conservative
(≈1.6× too wide against statsmodels in the reference design), and its ATT intervals carried the theoretical risk of
being anti-conservative (not observed in the designs tested). Intervals are Wald intervals (normal
quantiles) around the point estimate. No interval accounts for model selection after seeing
outcomes. Supplying a propensity treats it as the *true* assignment probability (see regime C).

**Reference / exactness checks.** With one binary confounder and saturated models, g-computation,
Hájek IPW and AIPW equal the hand-computed standardised estimate exactly (ATE and ATT); AIPW with no
covariates equals the difference in means with the unpooled SE; the AIPW risk ratio equals the
standardised risk ratio.

**Evaluation (200 independent M2 draws, evidence §7; coverages 0.93–0.94, each Wilson interval contains 0.95, smallest exact binomial p = 0.19 — not distinguishable from nominal, though 200 replications cannot exclude coverage a few points low).** Regime A AIPW bias −0.0012 ± 0.0016,
coverage 0.93; Hájek IPW −0.0012 ± 0.0016, 0.94; ATT AIPW −0.0009 ± 0.0016, 0.935; regime C
−0.0004 ± 0.0017, 0.93; naive +0.110; regime B +0.028 (coverage 0.78). At n = 20,000 (one draw) regime
A gives 0.087 [0.073, 0.102] and regime B 0.116 [0.102, 0.129].

**Canonical seed, reported as is.** On the default M2 build every valid estimate is 0.046–0.056
above 0.08 and most 95% intervals narrowly miss it, while both invalid post-treatment strategies
happen to cover it. Repeated sampling shows this is a ~2-SE draw, not bias; the evidence reports it
rather than choosing a flattering seed.

**Conditional effects.** `subgroup_effects` runs AIPW within each level of a discrete modifier;
`heterogeneity_test` is a Cochran Q test. On M2 the segment estimates (0.09, 0.16, 0.19) are
compatible with the true common 0.08 (Q = 2.46, p = 0.29). A constant risk difference implies
varying risk and odds ratios across baselines: heterogeneity is scale-dependent.

## 4. Diagnostics — `causal_inference/diagnostics.py`

| Diagnostic | Definition | M2 result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overlap | propensity quantiles by arm; share outside [0.05, 0.95]; common support | 0% outside; support [0.119, 0.898] |
| Balance | SMD with pooled *unweighted* SD (Austin 2009), raw and weighted; variance ratios | latent_value SMD 0.91 → 0.008; all weighted |SMD| < 0.06 |
| Weights | Kish ESS per arm, max weight, top-1% weight share, count above threshold | max 9.8; ESS 722/924 treated, 830/1076 control; no warnings |
| Propensity specification | estimated vs logged true propensity | max abs difference 0.073 |
| Trimming | `0.1 ≤ e ≤ 0.9` mask | changes the estimand to the trimmed-population effect |

Diagnostics cannot detect unmeasured confounding: regime B balances its own covariates perfectly
well and is still biased.

## 5. Negative controls and falsification

`log_income` is pre-treatment (the offer cannot affect it) but shares the confounder. Across
replicates the regime-A adjusted "effect" on it is +0.0006 ± 0.0013 (coverage of 0: 0.96) while
regime B gives +0.048 (flags residual confounding in ~80% of draws). On the canonical seed regime A's
estimate is −0.038 (a ~2-SE draw) and regime B's interval happens to include 0: a single negative
control is informative, not conclusive.

## 6. Sensitivity analysis — `causal_inference/sensitivity.py`

E-value `RR + sqrt(RR(RR − 1))` (VanderWeele & Ding 2017) for the point estimate and the CI limit
nearest 1; bias-formula grid `bias = γ·δ` for an unmeasured binary confounder on the risk-difference
scale (no U×T interaction; shifts, does not widen, the interval). Regime B: RR 1.29, **E-value 1.90**
(CI 1.68). The Ding & VanderWeele (2016) bounding factor
`B = RR_EU·RR_UD / (RR_EU + RR_UD − 1)` (with `B(E, E) = RR` defining the E-value) benchmarks the
actual omitted confounder, using its prevalence ratio between arms (RR_EU) and the max/min retention
ratio across its levels (RR_UD): with `latent_value` in 2 / 4 / 10 quantile bins, B = 1.17 / 1.43 /
1.85. A median split understates a continuous confounder; at finer resolution B exceeds the observed
RR, so in the worst case the bound describes the omitted confounder could explain the entire
estimate — the E-value analysis correctly flags regime B as not robust, while the actual bias is
smaller than that worst case (the effect is real). Benchmarks are marginal rather than conditional
on the proxies, and fine bins inflate max/min ratios through noise.

## 7. Failure cases — `causal_inference/scenarios.py` and the M2 campaign

| Failure | Mechanism | Result (evidence) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unmeasured confounding | regime B proxies leave `treated ← latent_value → churn` open | bias +0.028, coverage 0.78 |
| Post-treatment adjustment | adjusting for billing count (descendant of churn) | bias −0.044, coverage 0.20 |
| Post-treatment selection | analysing only customers billed at least once | bias −0.025, coverage 0.85 |
| Collider (M-bias) | adjusting for a pre-treatment collider with no confounding present | error −1.18 (true effect 1.0) |
| Mediator | adjusting for M estimates the direct effect (1.0), not the total (2.2) | error −1.20 vs total |
| Positivity | e = sigmoid(6x): min-arm ESS share 2.8%; AIPW RMSE 9.7 (misspecified outcome model + extreme weights) | trimming recovers the effect in the kept population (error +0.010) |
| Misspecification | Kang–Schafer design | AIPW unbiased if either model right (RMSE 1.67 / 0.24); both wrong: RMSE 26.6 |
| Interference | within-cluster spillovers | difference in means misses the policy effect by −2.0 |

## Interpretation and decision relevance

The offer works (true +8 points), but the naive comparison claims +19–23 points: anyone budgeting on
it would overpay for retention by a factor of about 2.5. With the confounder measured, adjustment
recovers the truth on average; without it, the best available adjustment still overstates the effect
by about a third, and only sensitivity analysis can say how fragile the estimate is. Validity comes
from the identification argument (DAG), not from the estimator's sophistication or a single
dataset's numbers.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic ground truth validates the code and the logic, not external validity.
- The M2 campaign is a time-invariant customer attribute: the generator applies the effect to every
  treated customer's retention irrespective of signup date relative to the campaign start, so the
  estimand is "effect of being a targeted customer", not of a dated intervention.
- The M2 DGP has a constant risk-difference effect; heterogeneous-effect estimation is exercised only
  in simulation (`scenarios.confounded`, `scenarios.positivity`).
- Parametric (main-effects logistic/linear) nuisance models only; no machine-learning nuisance
  models, cross-fitting or double machine learning.
- Influence-function SEs ignore nuisance estimation (conservative for Hájek ATE, not guaranteed for
  ATT); bootstrap SEs ignore model selection.
- Bias-formula sensitivity assumes a binary confounder without U×T interaction; E-value benchmarks
  in the evidence are marginal, not conditional, associations.
- Propensity-score matching not implemented (needs Abadie–Imbens inference) — open roadmap item.
- `valid_adjustment_sets` is exhaustive (≤ 16 candidate variables).
- Missing `region` (2%, MCAR in M2) is coded to the reference level; harmless here because region
  has no edge in the DAG, but not a general missing-data strategy.
