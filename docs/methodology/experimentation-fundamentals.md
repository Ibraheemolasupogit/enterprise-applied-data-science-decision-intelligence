# Experimentation fundamentals (Milestone 6)

Status: `IMPLEMENTED`, `TESTED`, `SIMULATED` as of Milestone 6 — see
[../evidence/capability-status.md](../evidence/capability-status.md). Numerical evidence:
[../evidence/m6-experimentation-fundamentals-results.md](../evidence/m6-experimentation-fundamentals-results.md)
(generated by `python scripts/generate_m6_evidence.py`, about three minutes). Section references
("results §n") point to that file.

Pillar 1 estimated effects from data the analyst did not control. An experiment *creates* the
contrast: randomisation makes assignment independent of the potential outcomes, so a difference in
means between assigned arms is an unbiased estimate of the effect of assignment with no adjustment
for confounders. That guarantee covers only the comparison **as randomised**. Most ways an
experiment goes wrong happen after randomisation — units lost, duplicated or excluded, exposure
that differs from assignment, outcomes that go missing, analyses restricted to subgroups the
treatment itself created — and M6 is as much about detecting and handling those as about the tests.

## Scope and boundaries

In scope: deterministic and seeded random assignment (unit, stratified and cluster level); sample
ratio mismatch detection; planning (power, sample size, MDE, duration, design effect); two-arm and
A/B/n analysis of binary and continuous metrics with absolute and relative effects; ITT,
noncompliance (complier effect) and attrition bounds; validity checks; multiple-testing corrections;
pre-specified decision rules with guardrails; the M2 experiment and an additive scenario generator.

Out of scope (M7, not implemented): CUPED and covariate adjustment, sequential testing and
always-valid inference, Bayesian experimentation, switchback/geo designs, interference and network
experiments, bandits, heterogeneous-effect analyses and long-term effect estimation. Peeking and
optional stopping are not demonstrated here: every analysis is fixed-horizon.

## Definitions fixed before the data are seen

`experimentation.design.ExperimentDesign` records — and validates — what an analysis plan must fix:

| Element | Meaning | M2 experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experimental unit | What receives the treatment | customer |
| Randomisation unit | What assignment is drawn for (equal to or coarser than the experimental unit) | customer |
| Analysis unit | What the analysis treats as independent | customer |
| Estimand | The causal quantity targeted | ITT: difference in 14-day conversion probability between assignment to the feature and to control, over eligible customers |
| Primary metric | The single metric the decision rests on | 14-day conversion |
| Minimum worthwhile effect δ | The smallest effect worth acting on (business threshold, not a statistical one) | +0.02 (break-even under stated economics) |
| Secondary metrics / guardrails | Supporting metrics; harms with non-inferiority margins | revenue, sessions / none in M2 |
| α, power, allocation, SRM threshold, exclusion rules | Error rates and design | 0.05, 0.8, 50/50, p ≤ 0.001, none |

`ExperimentDesign` refuses an analysis unit that differs from the randomisation unit unless a
clustered analysis is declared, and refuses guardrails without margins.

## 1. Assignment — `experimentation/assignment.py`

| Method | Mechanism | Guarantee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hash_assignment` | SHA-256 of `"{experiment}|{salt}|{unit_id}"` → uniform in [0, 1) → arm by cumulative weights | Deterministic, order-free, stable as units are added, independent between experiments; Bernoulli/multinomial arm sizes |
| `complete_randomisation` | Seeded permutation of a label vector | Fixed arm sizes; remainders allocated by systematic sampling so every unit has exactly probability `w_a` |
| `stratified_randomisation` | Complete randomisation within each stratum | Removes chance imbalance in the stratifier; analyse with `analysis.stratified_difference` |
| `cluster_randomisation` | Complete randomisation of clusters | Whole clusters share an arm; analysis must be clustered |

Tests verify the SHA-256 key, order invariance, stability, allocation ratios and independence
between experiments (χ² tests), exact marginal probabilities with remainders (Monte Carlo), within-
stratum balance and whole-cluster assignment.

## 2. Sample ratio mismatch — `experimentation/srm.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Question | Is the analysed sample consistent with the designed allocation? |
| Test | Pearson χ² goodness of fit against the design shares (any number of arms); exact binomial for two arms. Threshold p ≤ 0.001 (the check runs on every experiment; false alarms are costly; real SRMs are usually extreme). |
| Reference checks | Statistic and p-value equal `scipy.stats.chisquare`; exact p equals a direct binomial sum (tests). |
| Calibration | False-alarm rate at p ≤ 0.001: 0.003 [0.001, 0.009] over 1,000 hash-assigned experiments; at p ≤ 0.05: 0.067 (results §5). |
| **Detection vs diagnosis** | Equal losses produce equal detection whatever the cause — 5% loss is detected 0.963 (random) and 0.977 (outcome-related) of the time — but only outcome-related loss biases the estimate (−0.0001 vs +0.0069 against an effect of 0.018). At 2% outcome-related loss the test never fired in 300 experiments while the estimate was biased by +0.0026 (about 14% of the effect). |
| Diagnosis aids | `srm_breakdown` (per pre-treatment segment, Holm-adjusted) localises the imbalance; comparing the server-side assignment log with the analysed telemetry shows *which* units are missing and their pre-treatment profile. These generate hypotheses for an engineering investigation; they do not establish the cause. |
| Interpretation | SRM → HOLD: do not interpret the effect until the cause is found. No SRM → not proof of no damaging loss. |

## 3. Planning — `experimentation/planning.py`

Thin wrappers around the M3 power functions in experiment language. Binary metrics: pooled
two-proportion z-test power (normal approximation); continuous: two-sample t power with a common SD.
Options: absolute or relative MDE, unequal allocation, Bonferroni for `n` comparisons, daily traffic
→ duration, and the design effect `1 + ((cv² + 1) m − 1) ICC` (Eldridge, Ashby & Kerry 2006) for
cluster randomisation.

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Closed form | Per-arm n within 1 of Fleiss' formula (tests) |
| statsmodels | Continuous plans agree with `TTestIndPower` to within one unit per arm; MDEs to 1e-4 relative (tests) |
| Simulated power at planned n (1,000 experiments per design, results §6) | 0.786–0.797 against a target of 0.80; every Wilson interval contains 0.80 |
| M2, realised design | 566/569 customers → MDE 0.055 at 80% power (baseline 0.10); 1,035 per arm needed for the true +0.04, 3,841 for the worthwhile +0.02 |

Power is a design quantity. "Observed power" from the estimate is a transformation of the p-value
and is not reported.

**M3 defect found and fixed.** SciPy 1.18's noncentral-t CDF returns NaN deep in the lower tail
(e.g. `nct.cdf(-1.98, 98, 9)`), so M3's `power_two_sample_t` returned NaN for large noncentrality
and `minimum_detectable_effect_two_sample_t` failed at experiment-scale sample sizes (≥ ~1,000 per
arm). Fix in `statistics/power.py`: a NaN tail is replaced by 0 only when its exact upper bound
`P(T < 0) = Φ(−ncp)` is below 1e-12; otherwise an error is raised. Regression test against
statsmodels; all M3 tests and the M3 evidence are unchanged.

## 4. Analysis — `experimentation/analysis.py`

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Estimand | ITT: effect of *assignment*, units analysed as randomised. No adjustment is needed for identification. |
| Binary metrics | Risk difference with the Newcombe hybrid score interval and the pooled z-test (M3); relative lift from the Katz log risk-ratio interval (M3). |
| Continuous metrics | Welch t-test and interval (M3); relative lift by the delta method on `log(mean_t / mean_c)`; optional BCa bootstrap (M3, stratified by arm). |
| Stratified designs | `stratified_difference`: `Σ (N_s/N)(ȳ_t,s − ȳ_c,s)` with the Neyman variance — analysis as randomised; not covariate adjustment. |
| Clustered designs | `clustered_difference`: CR1 cluster-robust OLS with t(G − 1) (M5) and Welch on cluster means (a cluster-weighted estimand), beside the invalid naive analysis. Refuses clusters containing both arms. |
| A/B/n | Each arm vs control; Holm-adjusted p-values (FWER) and Bonferroni-level simultaneous intervals. Winner's curse in the selected arm's estimate is not corrected. |
| Missing outcomes | Refused: attrition must be handled explicitly (section 5). |
| Reference checks | p-values equal statsmodels `proportions_ztest` and SciPy Welch; Newcombe interval equals statsmodels `confint_proportions_2indep(method="newcomb")`; delta-method SE matches a simulated sampling SD; stratified estimator equals a manual computation; CR1 SE equals statsmodels cluster OLS (tests). |
| Calibration (results §4) | 300 populations of 20,000: conversion difference coverage 0.940 [0.907, 0.962], relative lift 0.943, revenue 0.937; stratified estimator 0.950. A/A false-positive rates at 20,000 users: 0.053 (z), 0.060 (Welch), 0.067 (BCa). |
| Small samples, heavy tails | With 100 users per arm the zero-inflated revenue metric makes Welch conservative (0.022) and the BCa bootstrap anti-conservative (0.144). |

### Rejection rule and the test/interval boundary

**p = α.** Every M6 test-based decision — `MetricResult.significant`, `SRMResult.detected`, the
SRM breakdown, the balance and exclusion checks, multiplicity corrections and the simulation
tallies — rejects iff `p ≤ α` (`statistics.multiple_testing.rejects`). This is the definition of the
p-value as the smallest level at which the test rejects, and the convention already used by the M3
simulation helpers and by statsmodels `multipletests`. With continuous statistics `p = α` has
probability zero; the rule matters only for exact ties, and keeps a valid test's size at most α.
Intervals are closed: "excludes 0" means `lower > 0` or `upper < 0`.

**Binary metrics: the pooled z-test and the Newcombe interval are not dual.** The test estimates the
variance under H0 (pooled); the interval combines two Wilson score intervals. Near the 5% boundary
they can disagree in either direction — 25/100 vs 14/100: p = 0.0496 but [−0.0004, +0.218];
168/200 vs 36/50: p = 0.0502 but [+0.0002, +0.264]. Neither is altered to match the other. Policy:
`significant` reports the test, `interval_excludes_zero` reports the interval, a disagreement is
flagged in the result's warnings (`test_and_interval_agree = False`), and the decision rule treats a
non-zero effect as **established only when both agree** — otherwise HOLD (section 8). Choosing
whichever method gives the preferred answer after seeing the data would inflate the error rate; the
conservative rule can only turn a borderline LAUNCH/ITERATE into HOLD. Welch's test and interval
are dual, so continuous metrics are unaffected (up to floating-point rounding).

### Statistical vs business significance

A p-value answers whether there is evidence of *some* effect. The decision needs the size of the
effect relative to δ and its uncertainty. The M2 experiment illustrates both failure directions:

* **Non-significant is not "no effect".** M2: +0.031 [−0.007, +0.069], p = 0.109. The truth is
  +0.04. Over 1,000 re-runs of the M2 generator, the test detects the real effect in 0.523 of
  experiments — matching the planned 0.507 power (results §2).
* **Significant estimates from underpowered designs are exaggerated.** Among significant positive
  replicates the mean estimate is 0.056 against the true 0.04 (type M error).
* **Ruling out a worthwhile effect** — not a large p-value — is evidence of no useful effect: the
  rule says STOP only when the interval's upper end is below δ.

## 5. Assignment vs treatment received, and attrition — `experimentation/compliance.py`

| Analysis | Estimand | Validity | Evidence (results §7) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ITT (by assignment) | Effect of assignment, diluted by noncompliance | Protected by randomisation | bias −0.0002 ± 0.0003, coverage 0.953 |
| CACE / Wald (`ITT_Y / ITT_D`) | Effect of exposure among compliers | Monotonicity, exclusion restriction (Angrist, Imbens & Rubin 1996); weak first stage warned (F < 10) | bias −0.0004 ± 0.0004, coverage 0.953 |
| As-treated (by exposure) | None (association) | Selection on post-randomisation behaviour | 0.064 vs ATE 0.018; coverage 0 |
| Per-protocol (compliant units) | None (association) | Same | 0.047 vs 0.018; coverage 0 |
| Complete-case under attrition | ITT among respondents | Biased if response relates to outcomes | +0.0060 ± 0.0004; coverage 0.805 |
| Lee (2009) bounds | ITT among always-observed units | Monotone response | contain target 100%; width 0.140 |
| Manski bounds | ITT, everyone | Bounded outcome only | contain target 100%; width 0.350 |

Reference checks: the Wald estimate equals two-stage least squares exactly; its delta-method SE
equals the 2SLS HC0 sandwich up to the per-arm `n/(n−1)` factor; Lee and Manski bounds match
hand-computed examples (tests). Lee bounds use fractional trimming (correct with tied, e.g. binary,
outcomes); their bootstrap interval takes the 2.5% quantile of the lower and 97.5% of the upper
bound (conservative for the identified set).

**Lee bootstrap failure policy.** Bounds are undefined when an arm has no observed outcomes
(`LeeBoundsUndefinedError`). A resample can produce that; such draws are never dropped or redrawn
(that would condition the bootstrap on response, the very selection the bounds address) and no
interval is fabricated. Before resampling, the probability that any of the B draws empties an arm,
bounded by `B · Σ_arms (1 − q_arm)^n_arm`, is computed from the data; above 1e-6 the bootstrap is
refused, so the outcome depends on the data rather than the seed. If an undefined draw still occurs
(probability ≤ 1e-6) the same error is raised. The point bounds remain available without the
bootstrap. In the M6 scenarios (thousands of units, ≥ 75% response) the bound is negligible. In the attrition scenario both bounds are too wide to
sign an effect of 0.018 — honest uncertainty under 10% vs 25% attrition.

## 6. Validity checks — `experimentation/validity.py`

| Check | FAIL when | WARN when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate units | a unit has conflicting arms (keep the first assignment) | identical duplicate rows |
| Sample ratio mismatch | p ≤ SRM threshold | — |
| Assignment leakage / noncompliance | — | control units exposed or treatment units unexposed beyond 1% |
| Pre-treatment balance | joint F-test of assignment on covariates p ≤ 0.001 (mechanism problem) | any \|SMD\| > 0.1 (chance; report, never re-randomise) |
| Sample exclusions | a rule uses post-randomisation information, or exclusion rates differ by arm | — |
| Metric integrity | non-finite or out-of-domain values | missing values (use bounds, not drops) |
| Post-randomisation selection | the variable is measured after assignment (a test cannot prove it is unaffected) | — |

The balance check uses the M4 `balance_table` (SMDs) and an M3 OLS F-test, valid under the
randomisation null. Results §10 summarises what each check can and cannot detect; interference
between units is not detectable by any of them. In the M2 experiment the balance check WARNs
(tenure SMD +0.166, joint p = 0.012): reported as chance imbalance, not "fixed".

Demonstrated failures (results §3, §8): restricting the analysis to users who "engaged" after
assignment biases the estimate by −0.0094 ± 0.0004 (about half the effect); analysing a
city-randomised A/A experiment per user rejects 0.346 (40 cities) or 0.596 (10 cities) of the time,
while CR1 rejects 0.042 / 0.072 and cluster-means Welch 0.036 / 0.042.

## 7. Multiple metrics — `statistics/multiple_testing.py`

Bonferroni and Holm (FWER), Benjamini–Hochberg and Benjamini–Yekutieli (FDR); adjusted p-values
equal statsmodels `multipletests` to 1e-12 (tests). With 10 independent metrics (3 real effects),
unadjusted testing makes at least one false claim in 29.3% of experiments; Holm 3.8% with power
0.275; BH controls the FDR (0.031) with power 0.326 (results §9). Correlated metrics, as in real
experiments, make Bonferroni/Holm conservative; BH needs independence or positive dependence.

## 8. Decision rules — `experimentation/decision.py`

Applied in order: validity FAIL → **HOLD**; guardrail harm beyond its margin (95% CI wholly above) →
**STOP**; benefit CI upper < δ → **STOP**; test and interval disagree about 0 (boundary, section 4)
→ **HOLD**; CI lower > 0, estimate ≥ δ and guardrails non-inferior → **LAUNCH**; CI lower > 0
otherwise → **ITERATE**; otherwise → **HOLD** (inconclusive). A non-zero effect is established
only when the test rejects (p ≤ α) and the interval excludes 0. Guardrails use
the two-sided 95% interval (one-sided 97.5% non-inferiority).

Operating characteristics at the sample size planned for δ = 0.01 (results §9): LAUNCH 0.0% at a
harmful −0.005, 0.3% at no effect, 51.0% at δ, 99.8% at 2δ. `business_impact` translates the
effect and its interval into annual value under stated assumptions (M2: −£40,613 to +£73,941 —
inconclusive in money as in probability).

## 9. Data

* **M2 experiment** (unchanged): 1,135 eligible customers, Bernoulli 50/50 assignment, exact
  individual effect +0.04 on conversion. Secondary metrics (14-day revenue and sessions) are derived
  from M2 transactions and sessions, which M2 generates independently of the experiment — their true
  effects are exactly zero. `latent_value` is never used.
* **`data/experiment_scenarios.py`** (new, additive): populations with coupled potential outcomes,
  heterogeneous effects by segment, revenue, guardrail metrics, clusters, compliance types, monotone
  response, post-randomisation loss (random, low-engagement, outcome-related), duplicate log rows
  and a post-randomisation "engaged" indicator. All randomness is drawn with the population; the
  realisation given an assignment is deterministic. Truths are expected effects averaged over the
  generated units. An integration test confirms the M2 build is unaffected.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic ground truth validates code and logic, not real-world validity; the scenario mechanisms
  are illustrative.
- Planning uses normal approximations; no exact binomial power (carried from M3).
- Relative lift intervals are delta-method/Katz approximations; no Fieller interval.
- A/B/n analysis does not correct the winner's-curse bias of the selected arm's estimate.
- The CACE needs monotonicity and the exclusion restriction, which the data cannot verify; Lee bounds
  need monotone response; both bounds can be uninformative under heavy attrition.
- The decision thresholds and the M2 economics are illustrative assumptions.
- No covariate adjustment, sequential monitoring, interference handling or heterogeneous-effect
  analysis (M7). Balance checks and SRM tests have limited power and cannot certify validity.
