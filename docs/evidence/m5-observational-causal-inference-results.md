## 1. Difference-in-differences on the canonical rollout

Canonical rollout (`RolloutConfig()`): 60 markets × 24 periods; cohorts (first treated period: markets) {0: 15, 9: 15, 13: 15, 17: 15}; AR(1) errors ρ = 0.5. Effects grow with exposure and are larger for early adopters. Timing: cohort g = first treated period, e = t − g, e = −1 is the reference.

**Estimand:** overall ATT = average effect over treated market-weeks = **4.028** (known by construction; read after estimation).

| estimator | estimate | 95% CI | inference | CI covers truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| static TWFE | 3.164 | [2.652, 3.676] | cluster-robust by unit (CR1, t with 59 df) | no |
| static TWFE, wild cluster bootstrap | 3.164 | [2.663, 3.656] | WCR Rademacher, 1999 draws | no |
| Callaway–Sant'Anna (never-treated) | 4.304 | [3.780, 4.829] | unit-level influence function (cluster-robust; conditional on cohorts) | yes |

**Why TWFE misses.** If `Y_it = α_i + λ_t + τ_it·D_it + ε_it` in a balanced panel, the TWFE coefficient is exactly Σ w_it·τ_it over treated market-periods plus a mean-zero noise term, with weights fixed by the adoption dates and panel length alone (not by the effects). Here 22% of treated market-periods (a count share) carry negative weight, totalling -0.047 of weight mass; with the true effects the weights imply 3.157, a *different estimand* from the overall ATT 4.028 — later adopters are compared with already-treated early adopters whose effects are still growing. The decomposition does not hold under non-parallel trends or spillovers, which add terms of their own. The wild bootstrap fixes the *uncertainty* of the coefficient, not its *meaning*.

Pointwise intervals must not be read jointly. The sup-t band (Montiel Olea & Plagborg-Møller 2019) is a *nominal* 95% simultaneous band; in this design it exhibits **undercoverage** — its observed joint coverage is below 0.95 and nominal coverage is not guaranteed. The shortfall is plausibly concentrated at the long horizons, which rest on one cohort of 15 markets (not separately verified).

(The PyPI `rdd` package's IK implementation returns about half this bandwidth because it floors the third-derivative estimate at m₃² ≥ 0.01, an ad-hoc and scale-dependent constant; development check.)
