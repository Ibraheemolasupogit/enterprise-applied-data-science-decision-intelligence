| Newcombe CI, true effect 0 | 0 | 5.56e-04 | +0.0006 ± 0.0006 | 0.01928 | 0.01886 | 0.01928 | 0.946 [0.930, 0.958] | 0.054 [0.042, 0.070] | 0.05 | 0.04573 |

Decision frequencies (worthwhile effect 0.02):

| true effect | LAUNCH | ITERATE | HOLD | STOP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +0.04 | 0.521 | 0 | 0.478 | 0.001 |
| +0.00 | 0.029 | 0 | 0.798 | 0.173 |

With a real +0.04 effect the test detects it in only about half of the experiments (matching the planned power), so a non-significant result is common even though the feature works. Significant replicates overstate the effect (mean 0.056 vs truth 0.04; type M error) — a consequence of underpowering. Under no effect the interval keeps nominal coverage and LAUNCH occurs only through an upward false positive (at most α/2), which in this design always comes with an exaggerated estimate.

## 7. Noncompliance and attrition

**Noncompliance** (300 experiments): 30% never-takers, 10% always-takers (control users exposed — leakage), compliance more likely among engaged users.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ITT (vs ITT truth) | 0.0108 | 0.01058 | -0.0002 ± 0.0003 | 0.004665 | 0.004608 | 0.004666 | 0.953 [0.923, 0.972] |
| CACE / Wald (vs complier effect) | 0.018 | 0.01763 | -0.0004 ± 0.0004 | 0.007771 | 0.007665 | 0.007772 | 0.953 [0.923, 0.972] |

The ITT (by assignment) and the Wald complier effect recover their own estimands. As-treated and per-protocol comparisons select on a post-randomisation behaviour correlated with outcomes and overstate the effect 2.6–3.6-fold.

**Attrition** (200 experiments): outcomes missing for 10% of control and 25% of treatment users, more often among the less engaged (monotone: treatment never prevents an outcome from being observed).

| estimator | truth (mean) | mean estimate | bias ± MC SE | RMSE | mean reported SE | empirical SD | 95% CI coverage [Wilson] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| complete-case difference (vs ITT) | 0.018 | 0.02403 | +0.0060 ± 0.0004 | 0.00795 | 0.005256 | 0.005193 | 0.805 [0.745, 0.854] |

| bounds | contain their target [Wilson] | mean width |
|---|---|---|
| Lee point bounds contain always-observed effect | 1.000 [0.981, 1.000] | 0.1404 |
| Lee bootstrap 95% CI contains it | 1.000 [0.981, 1.000] | — |
| Manski bounds contain ITT | 1.000 [0.981, 1.000] | 0.35 |

Complete-case analysis is biased upwards because treated respondents are a more engaged subset. Lee bounds target the effect among always-observed users (under monotone response); Manski bounds target the ITT for everyone with no assumptions and are wider still. Both always contain their targets here, but at widths of 0.140 and 0.350 against an effect of about 0.018 they cannot even sign the effect: with this much differential attrition, honest uncertainty is large. Neither is a point estimate.

## 9. Multiple metrics and decision rules

**Multiple metrics** (1000 experiments, 10 metrics of which 3 have a true effect of 0.1 SD, 1,000 users per arm, independent metrics):

| procedure | family-wise error [Wilson] | false discovery rate | average power |
|---|---|---|---|
| unadjusted | 0.293 [0.266, 0.322] | 0.1218 | 0.5983 |
| bonferroni | 0.034 [0.024, 0.047] | 0.01925 | 0.2677 |
| holm | 0.038 [0.028, 0.052] | 0.02058 | 0.2753 |
| benjamini-hochberg | 0.073 [0.058, 0.091] | 0.03148 | 0.326 |

Unadjusted testing makes at least one false claim in 29.3% of experiments. Holm controls the family-wise error with at least Bonferroni's power; Benjamini–Hochberg controls the false discovery rate (not the family-wise error, which it exceeds here) with more power still.

**Decision-rule operating characteristics** (1000 experiments per row; baseline 0.10, worthwhile effect +0.01, 14,751 per arm = 80% power for +0.01):

| true effect | LAUNCH | ITERATE | HOLD | STOP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -0.005 | 0 | 0 | 0.007 | 0.993 |
| 0 | 0.003 | 0.014 | 0.167 | 0.816 |
| 0.005 | 0.07 | 0.211 | 0.404 | 0.315 |
| 0.01 | 0.51 | 0.301 | 0.168 | 0.021 |
| 0.015 | 0.926 | 0.063 | 0.009 | 0.002 |
| 0.02 | 0.998 | 0.002 | 0 | 0 |

A useless change (true effect 0) is launched in 0.3% of experiments and a harmful one (−0.005) in 0.0%; a change exactly at the worthwhile effect launches 51.0% of the time (the estimate must reach the threshold), and clearly worthwhile effects launch reliably. ITERATE captures real but sub-threshold effects. The thresholds are a business choice; these frequencies are what that choice implies.

## 10. What the validity checks can and cannot detect

| validity threat | detectable from the data? | limits |
|---|---|---|
| sample ratio mismatch | yes, when large enough | detection says nothing about cause or bias; small damaging losses pass undetected (section 5) |
| duplicate / conflicting assignment | yes (assignment log) | first assignment kept; contamination reported |
| assignment leakage / noncompliance | yes, if exposure is logged | ITT stays valid for assignment; complier effect needs IV assumptions |
| pre-treatment imbalance | yes (chance vs mechanism) | chance imbalance is expected; never re-randomise post hoc |
| post-treatment exclusions / selection | only if variable timing is declared | the data cannot prove a variable is unaffected by treatment |
| differential attrition | rates yes; mechanism no | bounds, not complete-case estimates |
| metric integrity | domain and missingness checks | silent definition changes are not detectable from the data |
| interference between units | no (not in these checks) | requires design knowledge or cluster/switchback designs (M7) |
