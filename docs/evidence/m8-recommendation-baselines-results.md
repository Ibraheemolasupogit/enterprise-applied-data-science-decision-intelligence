# Milestone 8 evidence — numerical results

Generated by `python scripts/generate_m8_evidence.py`; do not edit by hand. All randomness is seeded; with the versions below every number reproduces exactly (§15 timings and the runtime table excepted). Narrative and limitations: [m8-recommendation-baselines.md](m8-recommendation-baselines.md); methodology: [../methodology/recommender-baselines.md](../methodology/recommender-baselines.md).

Three views are kept apart throughout: **logged feedback** (observable), the **synthetic oracle** (ground truth, read only after estimation) and **simulated online value** (the generator's click model — not an online experiment). Ground truth establishes nothing about real-world validity. Intervals are 95% confidence intervals.

## 2. Leakage: chronological vs random holdout, and invariance

Both protocols score **the same held-out clicks** (a random half of the test-window clicks; 1710 users). The invalid protocol adds the other half of the test-window clicks — interactions that happen after some held-out ones — to training. Inflation = mean per-user NDCG@10 difference (one-sample t, M3).

| model | ndcg_chronological | ndcg_random_holdout | recall_chronological | recall_random_holdout | inflation | p_value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| global popularity | 0.1328 | 0.1375 | 0.2297 | 0.2353 | 0.004704 | 1.07e-12 |
| item-kNN (k=50) | 0.1994 | 0.2156 | 0.3309 | 0.3436 | 0.01617 | 1.62e-07 |
| PureSVD (k=16) | 0.1689 | 0.1753 | 0.2781 | 0.2853 | 0.006348 | 0.08784 |

| model | max_abs_score_change |

| global popularity | 0 |
| item-kNN (k=50) | 0 |

Reading: the invalid protocol raises mean NDCG@10 for every model; the increase is significant at 5% for global popularity, item-kNN (k=50) and not for PureSVD (k=16) (the size depends on how much a model exploits co-occurrence with the leaked clicks). Models fitted under the chronological protocol are exactly invariant to post-cutoff data.

## 7. Counterexample: offline winner ≠ online winner

Offline (logged) improvement need not be online improvement. Under hype logging (selected after inspection; §16–17 test the pattern without that choice), paired differences (A − B) in each view; logged and oracle: per-user paired t; online and IPS: per-session differences with user-clustered SEs.

| contrast | logged NDCG@10 | oracle NDCG@10 | sim. online clicks/session | IPS clicks/session |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| content-based − global popularity | -0.1034 [-0.1148, -0.0919] | 0.1294 [0.1235, 0.1353] | 0.2996 [0.2826, 0.3165] | 0.3639 [0.0259, 0.7019] |
| PureSVD − item-kNN | -0.0308 [-0.0406, -0.0211] | 0.0109 [0.0073, 0.0144] | 0.0333 [0.0234, 0.0433] | 0.0174 [-0.0766, 0.1114] |

Reading: the logged winner is user-kNN; the simulated-online winner is PureSVD. Logged metrics reward agreeing with what the logging policy showed: popular items collect clicks because they are shown, so a popularity-like model looks good offline. Content-based lists, ranked 6 of 7 by logged NDCG, beat global popularity on simulated online value. IPS estimates from the same logs have the online sign in both contrasts but are noisy: the content-based − popularity IPS interval excludes 0 and the PureSVD − item-kNN IPS interval includes 0. On this seed under default logging the logged, oracle and simulated online rankings agree (§6), but that is seed-specific: the content-based vs popularity reversal also appears under default logging for other seeds (§13, §17).

Reading: the standard error is calibrated (its mean matches the spread of the estimates, and clustering by user changes it little because users rarely have many test sessions). Coverage is significantly below 95% for global popularity, item-kNN (k=50). The misses are almost all on one side — the estimate falls low while its SE falls with it (positive skew, estimate–SE correlation) — the known weakness of a normal interval for an importance-weighted mean, not an error in the variance formula. Bias with the true examination: at most 1.2 MC SE; with the estimated examination: at most 1.8 MC SE. Clipping at 50 biases the estimate downwards heavily. Ignoring position bias gives a bias of +1.0% of the value (+2.4 MC SE) for global popularity, -5.3% of the value (-19.0 MC SE) for item-kNN (k=50): small for a list whose items are similarly attractive, a larger underestimate for a list that puts its most attractive items first.
